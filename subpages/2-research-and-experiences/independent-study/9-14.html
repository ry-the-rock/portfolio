The History of Computing Education Research

Reading for September 14th, 2026

Reading Response

ACM Citation
[1] Mark Guzdial and Benedict du Boulay. 2019. The History of Computing Education Research. In Sally A. Fincher & Anthony V. Robins, eds. The Cambridge Handbook of Computing Education Research. Cambridge Handbooks in Psychology. Cambridge University Press, 11–39.

Summary + Main Points

Guzdial et al. [2019] escapes the expected genre of this independent study. While backed by reseach, the chapter does not arrive at any beneficial conclusions. Instead, it organizes important findings and developments to give a quick yet dense history of computing education.

Scope

The chapter's scope is purposefully limited in three ways: content, time, and what I will call perspective. For content, Guzdial et al. [2019] does not focus on computing topics themselves; rather, it concentrates on how students come to learn computing. Time is condensed to the year of the first recorded computing research efforts in 1967 to the year of the first International Computing Education Research (ICER) Conference in 2005. The final limitation, what I call perspective, is enhanced through the use of the three guiding lenses of "tools," "objectives," and "research methods" [1].
