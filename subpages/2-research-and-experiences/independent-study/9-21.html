Problem

Dance and body movement can be an outlet for personal, cultural, and emotional expression. This, combined with computing, can result in interdisciplinary experiences that benefit both user and observer alike. danceON (dance Object Notation) is a program specified in Payne et. al. that is used to create “artistic computational artifacts within culturally relevant dance experiences” [1].

The system is first used in a remote two week summer camp for young women of color. Specifically, it is a result of a design-based research collaboration with Stem From Dance (SFD), a community organization aiming to introduce young women of color to the overlaps between body expression and computing. Impressions

As someone who is constantly thinking about interdisciplinary studies and accessibility within the world of computing, this was a fascinating read. The idea and motivation behind the programming system itself is wonderful, but I felt that certain constraints, specifically the camp being online, should have been weighed more heaviliy during developing. Major roadblocks can be seen when considering this fully.
Technological Access Online learning highlighted one major discrepancy amongst students: access to technology at home. During September 2021, it was surveyed that 96% of schools provided digital devices such as laptops, tablets, or Chromebooks. However, only 70% and 49% of schools offered internet access at home or provided internet or hotspot access at a location other than home, respectively.
