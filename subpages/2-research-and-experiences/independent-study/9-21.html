Reading Response

Problem

Dance and body movement can be an outlet for personal, cultural, and emotional expression. This, combined with computing, can result in interdisciplinary experiences that benefit both user and observer alike. danceON (dance Object Notation) is a program specified in Payne et. al. that is used to create “artistic computational artifacts within culturally relevant dance experiences” [1].

The system is first used in a remote two week summer camp for young women of color. Specifically, it is a result of a design-based research collaboration with Stem From Dance (SFD), a community organization aiming to introduce young women of color to the overlaps between body expression and computing. During the design process, three core principles and six sub-goals were derived to outline the intentions of the project, shown below.

Goals

  1. Personally and Culturally Relevant
    • Low barrier to entry: Clearly, accessibly, and immediately incorporate learner interests, abilities, and the styles and cultures of dance they care about.
    • Wide room for expression: Enable learners to progress on meaningful projects in which they are not constrained and are able to express their complex identities, beliefs, passions,and interests.
  2. Creative Coding: Computing and art making are intertwined and bidirectional
    • Art motivates code: Ideas, music, and choreography present clear opportunities to code unique, reactive animations.
    • Code motivates art: The act of writing code and observing the results leads to new opportunities for creative expression through remixing the code and making new movements.
  3. Embodied Learning: Body and movement are engaging metaphors for learning important computing concepts
    • Data literacy: Make transparent the body position data captured by computers and/or sensors while providing an accessible interface to empower learners to understand, use,and manipulate their own data for the purposes of making art.
    • Machine learning: Help learners understand the limitations and biases embedded in the processes with which computers see them, and introduce learners to the process of training a machine.
